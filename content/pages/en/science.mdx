# What science says about onion and hair

People have rubbed onion juice into their scalp for a long time, and onion shampoo has become a social-media trend. We think you deserve to know what is actually known, and what isn't. Here it is, without spin.

## What's in an onion

Onion (*Allium cepa*) is one of the richest dietary sources of quercetin, a flavonoid, and it contains sulphur compounds, which are also what make you cry when you cut one. These are the substances researchers look at when they study onion and hair.

## The one human study

In 2002, dermatologists Sharquie and Al-Obaidi published a small study in the *Journal of Dermatology*. People with **alopecia areata**, an autoimmune condition that causes patchy hair loss, rubbed **raw onion juice** onto the bald patches twice a day for two months. A second group used tap water.

| | Onion juice | Tap water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participants | 23 | 15 |
| Regrowth after 6 weeks | 20 people (86.9%) | 2 people (13%, at 8 weeks) |

That looks impressive, but the study has serious limits:

- **It's small.** 38 people in total.
- **It wasn't blinded.** Patients and doctors knew who used onion juice, and the smell makes it impossible to hide.
- **Tap water isn't a real placebo.**
- **Alopecia areata often clears up by itself.** Many people with a few small patches regrow hair without any treatment, which makes a short study hard to read.
- **It has never been repeated at scale.** Twenty-plus years later, it is still the study everyone cites.

## Lab and animal research

A 2023 study in *Protein & Cell* (Zhao et al.) found that quercetin applied to the skin of mice pushed resting hair follicles into their growth phase and helped older mice regrow hair, apparently by stimulating small blood vessels around the follicle. That's interesting biology. It is also mice, and pure quercetin, not onion and not people.

## What this means for our shampoo

- **No study has tested onion shampoo.** The evidence is about raw juice left on the scalp, for a medical condition. A shampoo is rinsed off after a minute or two, and contains an extract, not juice.
- **Our shampoo is a cosmetic.** It is made to clean and care for your hair and scalp. We don't claim it prevents or treats hair loss, and you shouldn't buy it for that.
- **If you're losing hair, see a doctor or dermatologist.** Hair loss has many causes, and some need medical treatment.
- **Onion can irritate.** Some people react to onion on their skin. Stop using any product that stings, itches or reddens your scalp.

We'll add to this page when there's better evidence, including any testing of our own formula.
